# PMS report discovery plan

## Purpose

Learn how real hotels produce and use PMS reports before choosing a parser,
adapter, schema change, or automation. Discovery is complete when Staxis can
describe each observed workflow, explain every required field, and make an
evidence-backed decision to build a shared adapter, build a property-specific
adapter, continue observing, or decline support.

This plan does **not** authorize parser code, schema changes, report-derived
writes, CUA/browser automation, production-data changes, or deployment changes.

## Fixed boundaries

- The existing report inbox remains intake-only: verified email, per-property
  recipient, sender approval, content-addressed receipt, private raw storage,
  upload verification, deduplication, and retention. It does not parse or
  classify reports.
- A report belongs to the `property_id` resolved by the server from its inbox
  address. Never infer or redirect ownership from a filename, subject, hotel
  name, sheet, or parser output.
- PMS-reported facts and Staxis-owned workflow remain separate. A future report
  adapter may write PMS mirror facts; it must never write `room_work` or replace
  a staff member's assignment, DND choice, checklist, note, or work status.
- Production report bytes stay in the private `pms-raw` boundary. Do not attach
  them to source control, tickets, chat, email threads, test fixtures, or local
  developer folders.
- A receipt may outlive its raw bytes. Discovery must respect each property's
  retention policy and must not extend retention silently.
- Staxis remains English-only. A non-English report is recorded as unsupported
  evidence and triggers a product decision; discovery does not add translation.

## Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery lead | Runs observations, maintains the evidence matrix, and records unknowns without guessing. |
| Hotel sponsor | Authorizes observation and samples for one property and confirms the people who may explain them. |
| Hotel operator | Demonstrates the actual night-audit/front-desk/housekeeping workflow and validates field meaning. |
| Security/privacy reviewer | Approves handling, access, redaction, retention, and any exceptional specimen use. |
| Future adapter owner | Makes the adapter decision only after the discovery gates pass; does not reinterpret hotel semantics alone. |

Consent is property-specific and purpose-specific. Record who approved the
observation, which report families are in scope, allowed viewers, retention,
and withdrawal contact. Consent never permits collection of credentials, full
card data, or unrelated guest information.

## Coverage strategy

Start with hotels, not vendor marketing material. Build a coverage matrix using
these dimensions:

- property and management company;
- PMS vendor, product, version/edition, hosting model, and enabled modules;
- branded/independent, select/full service, room count, and operating model;
- hotel timezone, DST behavior, night-audit cutoff, and business-date rule;
- report delivery method: scheduled email, manual export, shared mailbox, or no
  export at all;
- where the usable data appears: attachment, email body, authenticated link, or
  a report combining several properties;
- format: CSV, text, PDF, XLS, XLSX, multi-sheet, and multiple attachments;
- report purpose and hotel role that consumes it;
- schedule, expected arrival window, correction/backfill behavior, and source
  of truth when values disagree.

Do not assume that two hotels on the same PMS have the same report. Templates,
modules, column choices, locale settings, custom fields, and audit timing may
differ. Conversely, structurally identical reports from different brands may
belong to one adapter family.

A reusable adapter family needs evidence from at least two independent
properties with matching structure and semantics. One property can justify only
a property-specific candidate. These are minimum evidence rules, not promises
to build.

## Observation sequence

### 1. Qualify the hotel

Before collecting anything:

1. Confirm the property, hotel sponsor, approved observers, and approved report
   families.
2. Record the PMS product/version and whether reports are generated by the PMS,
   a brand system, a management-company tool, or a person.
3. Confirm that observing/exporting the report is permitted by the hotel's and
   vendor's policies.
4. Agree on handling and raw-file retention. If the hotel has not chosen a
   retention period, do not change the current setting as part of discovery.
5. Confirm that no Staxis or vendor credentials will be shared with the
   discovery team.

### 2. Observe the real workflow

Watch the responsible hotel employee perform the normal process. Do not lead
them through an imagined Staxis flow. Record:

- who creates, schedules, receives, opens, and acts on the report;
- every click or manual step, including filters and saved templates;
- report name in the UI versus filename, subject, sheet name, and visible title;
- whether the sender is the PMS, brand, management company, or a hotel employee,
  and whether forwarding changes the verified sender domain;
- frequency, scheduled local time, typical and worst arrival time;
- whether the report is pre-audit, post-audit, intraday, or ad hoc;
- which business date it represents and how the employee knows;
- what happens on missing, late, duplicate, partial, corrected, or re-sent
  reports;
- how staff decide which version is authoritative;
- values copied into another sheet/system and decisions made from them;
- reports that are available but deliberately not used.

Observe at least three normal report cycles per property before calling a shape
stable. Also observe or obtain an operator-described example of a late,
corrected, missing, or re-run cycle. Label descriptions that were not directly
observed as testimony, not fact.

### 3. Inventory report specimens

Use the existing inbox when a hotel already sends scheduled reports to Staxis.
The discovery record references only the property ID, receipt ID, content hash,
MIME type, byte size, received time, verified sender domain, approval state,
and retention state. Do not copy raw bytes out of the private boundary.

If an approved reviewer must inspect a production specimen, use an approved
service-role/admin access mechanism that keeps the file inside the private raw
boundary and exposes it for the minimum necessary viewing window. If no such
mechanism is available, pause discovery; creating a new download surface is a
separate security-reviewed task. Never create a permanent convenience copy.

Test fixtures for future work must be synthetic or irreversibly redacted inside
an approved private environment. A redacted derivative may leave that
environment only after an independent privacy review. It must remove guest
names, addresses, email, phone, loyalty
IDs, reservation/confirmation IDs, free text, employee personal data, payment
data, QR/barcodes, hidden sheets, workbook metadata, comments, formulas with
embedded identifiers, and PDF metadata. Preserve only the structural feature
being studied. Hashes of redacted files are new specimen identities; never
present them as the production receipt's hash.

If cardholder data or authentication material is seen or suspected, stop
inspection immediately. Do not depend on future automated detection. Notify the
security owner so an authorized operator can invoke the existing
quarantine/purge process; a discovery reviewer must not manipulate production
receipts or storage. Retain only the permitted non-sensitive receipt/audit
record.

### 4. Define report and field semantics

For every report family, record:

- report purpose, row grain, grouping, filters, subtotals, grand totals, and
  whether rows are a snapshot, event stream, or cumulative period;
- source business date, property timezone, night-audit boundary, printed as-of
  time, email received time, and which one controls freshness;
- raw field label and position, type, unit, currency, sign convention, allowed
  values, precision, and identifier stability;
- the difference between blank, zero, `N/A`, unknown, suppressed, and not
  applicable;
- whether a field is PMS-entered, calculated, manually overridden, or inferred;
- correction rules, restatements, cancellation/no-show lifecycle, and how a
  later report supersedes an earlier one;
- precedence when complementary report families describe the same reservation,
  room, or business date;
- the hotel role that can explain the field, the hotel role that acts on it,
  and the system considered authoritative when sources disagree;
- privacy class and whether Staxis actually needs the field.

Never infer a business date from email receipt time when the report supplies a
different date. Never use UTC calendar date as the hotel business date. When
the report does not make its business-date rule unambiguous, leave it unknown.

For housekeeping, explicitly classify each candidate field as:

- **PMS fact:** room identity, PMS housekeeping status, occupancy/reservation
  facts, or a name printed by the PMS; or
- **Staxis workflow:** assigned staff ID, DND override, checklist, note, start
  time, completion, inspection, or another action performed in Staxis.

Any proposed mapping from a report into the second category fails discovery.

### 5. Exercise variation and failure cases

For each candidate family, seek evidence for:

- reordered, renamed, inserted, omitted, or duplicated columns;
- extra headings, page breaks, footers, subtotals, blank rows, and multi-sheet
  workbooks;
- locale and formatting differences for dates, decimals, currency, room
  numbers, leading zeros, and text encoding;
- duplicate delivery, identical bytes under a new filename, and one email with
  multiple attachments;
- partial/truncated files, password-protected files, corrupt workbooks, image
  PDFs, unexpected MIME types, and oversized messages;
- late night audit, DST transitions, manual reruns, historical backfills,
  corrections, and reports arriving out of order;
- PMS upgrades, template edits, module changes, ownership changes, and a hotel
  changing its operating process.

Discovery records expected handling as `accept`, `reject`, `quarantine`,
`duplicate`, `supersede`, `manual review`, or `unsupported`. It does not build
that handling.

### 6. Group candidate adapters

Group reports by observed contract, not vendor name alone. A candidate adapter
contract includes structural fingerprint, required fields, semantic version,
business-date extraction, property binding, total reconciliation, correction
behavior, reject conditions, and supported variations.

Choose one disposition:

1. **Shared adapter candidate:** at least two properties demonstrate the same
   structure and semantics.
2. **Property-specific candidate:** stable and understood, but observed at only
   one property or materially customized.
3. **Continue discovery:** evidence is incomplete or contradictory.
4. **Unsupported:** unsafe, prohibited, non-repeatable, or incompatible with
   Staxis invariants.

Vendor-specific adapters may share internal primitives later, but discovery
must not force unlike report contracts into one universal parser.

## Adapter implementation gate

No parser or adapter work begins until all of the following are true for its
declared support boundary:

- Hotel consent, property ownership, sender identity, handling, and retention
  are recorded.
- The real delivery workflow is repeatable without sharing credentials or
  reviving browser automation.
- Required reports have at least three normal cycles per property and the
  evidence threshold for shared/property-specific classification is met.
- Grain, required fields, identifiers, units, null behavior, totals, and every
  mapped field's meaning have operator confirmation.
- Hotel-local business date, timezone, night-audit cutoff, as-of time, and
  freshness behavior are unambiguous.
- Missing, late, duplicate, out-of-order, corrected, and backfill behavior is
  specified.
- Required values reconcile to visible source totals, or the difference is
  explicitly understood and accepted.
- PMS facts can be written without touching `room_work` or another Staxis-owned
  workflow field.
- A synthetic/redacted fixture set can cover success, variation, correction,
  and hard rejection without retaining production PII.
- Unknown or malformed input has a fail-closed outcome; partial success cannot
  silently look complete.
- The adapter decision record names an owner, version boundary, rollout cohort,
  observable health signal, rollback condition, and unresolved non-blockers.

Passing this gate authorizes a separate implementation proposal and review. It
does not itself authorize code, migrations, or production rollout.

## Stop conditions

Stop collection or adapter consideration when any of these applies:

- hotel consent, vendor permission, property ownership, or authorized viewers
  are unclear;
- a report contains or may contain card data, credentials, security answers, or
  sensitive data outside the approved purpose;
- obtaining the report requires Staxis to hold a person's credentials, bypass
  access controls, or restore the retired CUA/browser robot;
- the report cannot be bound to exactly one property before inspection;
- one file combines multiple hotels, or an authenticated report link would
  require Staxis to fetch data outside the approved inbox boundary;
- business date, timezone, row grain, required-field meaning, or authoritative
  correction behavior remains ambiguous;
- a source is manual/non-repeatable but the proposed adapter assumes a reliable
  schedule;
- totals cannot be reconciled, a malformed file would be partially accepted,
  or required data is omitted without a detectable failure;
- the design would let report data overwrite `room_work` or other staff-owned
  state;
- the only evidence is a vendor demo, documentation, one unverified sample, or
  testimony with no observable cycle;
- a report is non-English and support would require localization;
- a new shared schema, permission change, production-data rewrite, or broader
  integration is needed. Record that as a separate proposal rather than
  expanding discovery.

Stopping is a valid result. Record the reason and the evidence needed to resume.

## Discovery acceptance criteria

Discovery for a candidate adapter family is complete only when it produces:

- a hotel/PMS/workflow coverage matrix with observed gaps;
- one hotel observation record per participating property;
- a specimen manifest referencing private receipts without copying raw data;
- an operator-confirmed semantic dictionary for every required field;
- normal, late, missing, duplicate, correction, and backfill behavior;
- a property/timezone/business-date and data-ownership decision;
- a security, consent, redaction, access, and retention record;
- a completed adapter decision with `shared`, `property-specific`, `continue`,
  or `unsupported` disposition;
- explicit unknowns, stop conditions, and evidence required to resume.

The templates in `docs/assets/pms-report-discovery/` are the working format.
Blank templates belong in source control; completed records and report-derived
artifacts belong only in an approved private evidence location.
